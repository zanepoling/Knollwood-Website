History

Knollwood remains one of Knoxville's oldest and most recognized historic landmarks, with distinctions awarded by the U.S. National Register of Historic Places and the Daughters of the American Revolution.

The land was first granted to Knoxville founder James White in the 1780s. White later sold a portion of the land grant to John Reynolds in 1817.

Samuel Frazier, a veteran of the American Revolution and a signer of the Tennessee Constitution, briefly occupied a home on the property before his death in 1839.

John's son, Major Robert Reynolds, inherited the property and built the Antebellum mansion later named "Knollwood," beginning in 1848. The original home was completed in 1851 at a time when Knoxville's population was only 2,076.

During the siege of Fort Sanders in 1863, General James Longstreet used Knollwood as a military headquarters. The home survived the remainder of the Civil War with no documented damage.

Robert Hank Edington, a Union Army veteran and railroad industrialist, occupied the home beginning in 1888.

Knollwood was subsequently owned by prominent Knoxville families until resting in our care in 2021. We intend for it to remain an East Tennessee treasure for generations to come.

Restoration

Brought back by hand.

In 2022, we began planning, demoing, restoring, and rebuilding Knollwood to become what it is today. Much of the original home is unchanged from 1851, while other portions have been meticulously restored or reimagined to honor the history of the site and to accommodate future guests.

Over 300 skilled craftsmen contributed their time and talent to this effort. We recognize each of them by name here and with a bronze monument installed near the rear entry of Knollwood. We are grateful to each of them.

Stunning Views

Sitting at an elevation of 1,040 feet atop the crest of Bearden Hill, Knollwood overlooks much of Knoxville and the picturesque Great Smoky Mountains of East Tennessee.

On a clear day, guests enjoy panoramic views of Clingmans Dome (38 miles), Mount LeConte (37 miles), and the ridge lines between Tennessee and North Carolina (40 miles).

Rooms

Comprising of 6,760 square feet of finished space, Knollwood has been restored and renovated to accommodate gatherings large and small. The main level rooms are outfitted to provide flexible and memorable meeting spaces for guests.

Gardens

The grounds at Knollwood were reimagined and now include an outdoor covered meeting veranda, a private courtyard, an observation terrace, tiered gardens, a grotto for outdoor gatherings, fountains, seasonally adorned planters, and dozens of plant varieties.

Location

Knollwood is located adjacent to Interstate 40 and Interstate 75 at Exit 383 (Papermill Dr.). Only 5.5 miles from downtown Knoxville and 4 miles from the University of Tennessee, it is conveniently located and accessible to the East Tennessee community.

Knollwood’s stunning architecture and adorned flagpole can be seen from the Interstate to the North or from as far away as Lakeshore Park to the South. While located in a commercial district and surrounded by restaurants, office buildings, and modern infrastructure, Knollwood’s high elevation, privacy walls, water features, and landscaping thoughtfully create an atmosphere of peaceful solemnity.
